01Forecasting + constrained optimization

Demand & Inventory Decision Lab

What should the next purchase order contain?

A retail decision study that links SQL analysis, demand forecasts and purchasing constraints, then tests whether a more sophisticated policy actually helps.

PythonSQL / DuckDBHiGHSMILP

Actual project output: forecast backtest. WAPE is forecast error; lower is better. MA8 was selected on validation, not on the test window.

Model scope80 SKUs
Held-out evaluation26 weeks
DataReal UCI retail sales
The useful finding

A better forecast does not automatically produce a better purchasing decision. The value of optimization depends on the constraints and the strength of the baseline.

Units sold are a proxy for demand. Costs, storage and lead times are simulated. The default-budget profit gain is not established: its moving-block bootstrap interval includes zero. No realized business savings are claimed.

  1. Prepare sales data and separate training, validation and test periods.
  2. Compare demand forecasts, then evaluate reorder and optimization policies on the same inputs.
  3. Constrain purchasing by budget, storage and integer case packs; inspect sensitivity and uncertainty.
Complete analysis package · code publication pending
02Geospatial analytics + operations research

Alexandria Service Siting

Where should five service locations go?

An auditable location-allocation study that combines population estimates and modeled road travel with exact optimization, rather than choosing sites by intuition.

PythonOR-Tools / SCIPWorldPopOSM / OSRM

Actual project map and method comparison. Coverage refers to the modeled population within 10 modeled travel minutes, not observed real-world response times.

Demand representation605 points
Candidate locations380 sites
Five-site modeled coverage88.0%
The useful finding

The exact model reaches 88.0% coverage versus 86.4% for the greedy baseline. The useful story is a proven answer inside a stated model, not an exaggerated improvement claim.

Educational prototype. Population is based on WorldPop 2020; travel times are modeled. Capacity and cost assumptions in the extensions are illustrative, not deployment specifications.

  1. Aggregate population estimates and construct a site-to-demand road-time matrix.
  2. Solve a mixed-integer maximum-coverage model, then compare it with sensible and naive baselines.
  3. Extend the study with capacity assignments and budget-constrained facility tiers.

03Evidence-first defensive monitoring

Cyber Sentinel

Can every alert explain why it fired?

A defensive authentication-log monitoring prototype with named detection rules, traceable scores, raw evidence and an analyst triage workflow.

The useful finding

Detection should be reviewable: an alert needs evidence and an explanation, not just a high number or a red label.

Synthetic, single-host authentication-log prototype. Rule-based, not machine learning. Detection-only, with no antivirus, blocking or production SOC claim.
